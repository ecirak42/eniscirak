The pattern

The symptom is scary. The fear of the symptom is what keeps the loop alive.

A wave of adrenaline hits.

Your heart races. Your chest tightens. You feel dizzy, hot, shaky, unreal, disconnected, or flooded with fear.

Then the mind tries to protect you: “What if something is wrong?” “What if I lose control?” “What if this feeling never goes away?”

That fear sends another danger signal through the body. More adrenaline comes. The sensations get louder. Then you start checking, resisting, avoiding, scanning, and bracing.

Before long, you are not only afraid of panic. You are afraid of your own inner experience. That is the loop this workshop is designed to help you understand.

Why I teach this

I’m not teaching this from theory alone.

My name is Enis Cirak. I lived inside this loop for years.

Panic attacks. Depersonalization. Intense anxiety. The quiet habit of scanning my body, planning around sensations, and trying to make sure I would not get “triggered” at the wrong time.

That experience changed the way I teach.

I do not see panic as weakness. I do not see it as a character flaw. And I do not think the answer is to force yourself harder, shame yourself into exposure, or try to positive-think your way out of something your body is deeply reacting to.

I see panic as a learned pattern in the nervous system — one that involves adrenaline, thoughts, sensations, emotional pressure, avoidance, and the way we relate to our inner experience.

This workshop is designed to help you see that pattern clearly, so you can begin practicing differently.

What you’ll learn

The pieces of the panic loop most people never connect.

Why panic feels so convincing

You’ll learn why panic can feel dangerous even when there is no immediate danger — and why the body can sound the alarm before the mind understands what is happening.

How adrenaline creates scary sensations

Racing heart, dizziness, tingling, heat, shaking, chest tightness, unreality, and fear surges can all become more frightening when you do not understand the adrenaline cycle.

Why thoughts and sensations amplify each other

A sensation appears. The mind interprets it. The interpretation creates more fear. The fear creates more sensation. This is the feedback loop.

How DP/DR fits into the picture

Depersonalization and derealization can feel terrifying, but they often make more sense when understood through overload, fear, scanning, resistance, and nervous-system protection.

Where emotion may play a role

Unfelt emotion, internal conflict, chronic stress, and the felt sense of “stuck energy” can keep the system braced. We’ll explore this gently, without turning it into another thing to fear.

Why avoidance makes sense — and why it can shrink your life

Avoidance can feel protective in the short term, but over time it can teach the nervous system that more and more places, sensations, or situations are unsafe.

How to practice exposure without forcing yourself

Exposure is not about white-knuckling through terror. It is about helping the nervous system relearn safety with steadiness, patience, and the right level of challenge.

FAQ

Questions people often ask

Is this therapy?

No. This is an educational coaching workshop. It is not therapy, diagnosis, treatment, crisis support, or medical advice. It may complement therapy or personal growth work, but it does not replace care from a licensed mental-health professional.

Will this stop my panic attacks?

This workshop is not a guarantee or a medical treatment. The goal is to help you understand the panic loop, reduce fear around symptoms, and begin practicing responses that may help the pattern soften over time.

Is this good for depersonalization/derealization?

DP/DR will be discussed as part of the panic/anxiety loop. The focus is on understanding why unreality feelings can show up when the nervous system feels overloaded, and how to relate to those sensations with less fear and resistance.
