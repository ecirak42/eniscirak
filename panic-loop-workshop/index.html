02

What usually keeps the cycle going

Panic often continues because of a very understandable loop.

A sensation shows up.

Your mind tries to figure out if something is wrong.

That fear adds more adrenaline.

The symptoms get louder.

You check, brace, escape, avoid, or look for reassurance.

You feel better for a moment.

Then your brain learns: “Good thing we escaped. That must have been dangerous.”

So the next time, the alarm comes on faster.

This does not mean you are broken. It means your system has learned a pattern.

The good news is that patterns can be worked with.

03

The panic cycle

1

A sensation appears

Racing heart, dizziness, tight chest, heat, tingling, shakiness, breath changes, or unreality.

2

Your mind reacts

“What if something is wrong?” “What if I lose control?” “What if this never goes away?” “What if I panic here?”

3

Fear rises

Your body adds more adrenaline, even if the situation itself is not dangerous.

4

The symptoms get stronger

Now the feeling seems like proof that something really is wrong.

5

You try to get safe

You check, brace, escape, avoid, seek reassurance, or try to force the feeling away.

6

The world gets smaller

You feel relief in the moment, but the fear has more control the next time.

In the workshop, you’ll map how this cycle works for you.

About me

My name is Enis Cirak.

I had panic attacks, depersonalization, and bad anxiety for about four or five years.

It changed how I lived. I started avoiding social situations and meetings. I got paranoid about trying new foods. I was overstimulated most of the time and didn’t feel like myself. The calm, confident version of me felt far away.

I know what it’s like to plan your day around whether your body is going to cooperate. I know what it’s like to sit in a room full of people and feel like you’re the only one who can’t just be there.

What got me out wasn’t numbing it, fighting it, or outrunning it. It was turning toward it. When you actually turn toward what you’re feeling, instead of bracing against it, it starts to move on its own.

That’s the work I do with people now. I remember how painful it is to watch your world get smaller, and I want to help other people move through it with more clarity and steadiness.

We go at your pace. No rushing, no pushing. Just honest conversation and real work.

Influences: Internal Family Systems, Michael Singer, Eckhart Tolle, Adyashanti, David Burns.

This workshop is a focused version of that work, centered on panic, DP/DR, avoidance, and the cycle that can build around symptoms.

FAQ

Is this therapy?

No. This is an educational coaching workshop. It is not therapy, diagnosis, treatment, or emergency care.

Will this stop my panic attacks?

No one can promise that. The workshop is meant to help you understand the cycle and begin responding to it differently.

Is this good for DP/DR?

Yes. We’ll talk about depersonalization and derealization as part of high anxiety and panic, especially how fear and checking can make those feelings more distressing.

Is this for agoraphobia or avoidance?

Yes. We’ll talk about how avoidance develops and how to start working with it in a gradual, honest way. If your symptoms are severe or disabling, it may also be important to work with a licensed professional.
