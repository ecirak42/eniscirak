The part nobody explained to me

Every test came back normal. Everyone kept telling me I was fine. Nobody explained why my body kept doing it anyway.

Here’s what I eventually learned. Panic runs on a loop. A sensation shows up. Fear spikes and floods you with adrenaline, which makes the sensation louder. You escape, and the relief teaches your brain it was a real danger. So your brain starts watching for the next one.

And watching finds things. A skipped beat. A light head. A moment where the room feels unreal. That’s why attacks seem to come from nowhere. The watching finds them first.

Once I understood this, everything I’d been doing to protect myself looked different. The checking, the escaping, the avoiding. All of it was feeding the loop.

A quick note, in case this page set something off

Reading about symptoms can start the checking. If something in your body just got loud, you don’t have to fix it. Let it be there while you keep reading. That’s the skill, and it’s learnable.
